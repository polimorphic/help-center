---
title: "A staff member can't log in"
---

When a coworker can't sign in, start by asking one question: can they not get in at all, or do they get in but can't open a page? The answer points you to the right fix below.

## They don't have access to your organization

Each person needs an account that's been invited to your organization. If they were never invited, or they were removed, sign-in fails or they land in the wrong place.

**Fix:** An administrator checks **Users** in settings and invites the person again if needed. See [Invite staff and manage accounts](/administration/invite-staff-and-manage-accounts).

## They signed in but can't open a page

This is an access problem, not a sign-in problem.

**Fix:** Follow I don't see a feature or button.

## They're not in the right single sign-on group

With single sign-on ([SSO](/getting-started/glossary)), your identity provider, such as Microsoft Entra ID, decides who gets in and which groups they join. Groups managed by SSO show a lock icon. You can't add people to them in Polimorphic.

**Fix:** Ask your IT team to add the person to the right group in your identity provider. Once that's done, have them sign in again.

## The emailed link expired

Sign-in links from **Sign in without a password** and password reset links stop working after a while.

**Fix:** Request a new email and use the newest link.

## The new password is rejected

Each organization can set its own password rules. If someone belongs to two organizations, their password has to meet the stricter set.

**Fix:** Follow the checklist under the password box. Once every rule is checked off, the password is accepted.

## The reset email doesn't arrive

**Fix:** Check the spelling of the email address and the spam or junk folder. Ask IT whether email from Polimorphic is being blocked.

## Their session expired

**Fix:** Sign in again. Your organization may set how long a session lasts.

## When to contact support

If none of these fixes work, contact support. Include the person's email address, organization, sign-in method (SSO or password), the exact error message, and the time it happened. Never send a password.
