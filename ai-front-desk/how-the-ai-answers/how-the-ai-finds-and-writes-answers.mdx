---
title: "How the AI finds and writes answers"
---

When a resident gets a surprising answer, the first question is usually "where did that come from?" The [AI Front Desk](/getting-started/glossary) answers by searching the sources your organization gives it, then writing a reply from what it finds. It only knows what's in your knowledge sources.

Once you know where answers come from, you can fix a wrong one at its source instead of guessing, and you'll have a good idea of what residents will hear before you go live.

## How an answer comes together

![Flow diagram with five boxes from left to right: Resident asks a question, AI searches your knowledge sources, AI looks up details if needed such as a map layer or connected system, AI writes an answer from what it found, Answer shows its sources or offers a person or a form.](/images/articles/56918030725019.png)

### A resident asks a question

Questions come in through the chat widget, the search bar on your website, a text message, or a phone call. All of them use the same knowledge.

### The AI searches your knowledge sources

The AI runs several searches at once and pulls the most relevant passages. It matches on meaning, so a resident who asks about "trash" can find a page about "solid waste collection." Sources can include:

- Pages from your website and other links
- Uploaded documents, such as a fee schedule
- [FAQs](/getting-started/glossary) (frequently asked questions) you write
- [Workflows](/getting-started/glossary) residents can apply through
- Transfer lines for phone calls
- [GIS](/getting-started/glossary) (geographic information system) map layers

Residents only get answers from sources set to **Everyone**. Sources set to **Staff only** are used only in staff tools. Archived sources aren't used at all.

### The AI looks up details when needed

Some answers depend on the person asking. If the question depends on location, such as "When is my recycling day?", and you have a matching map layer, the AI asks for an address and checks the layer. If your organization has a connected system, the AI may look up details there, such as a bill balance. These lookups are called tools.

### The AI writes the answer

The AI writes a reply from the passages it found. It gives phone numbers, email addresses, and street addresses only when they appear word for word in a source. Every link in an answer is checked against the sources. A link that doesn't match a source is removed.

### The answer shows sources or offers next steps

Web pages and documents appear as sources the resident can open. FAQs, transfer lines, and map layers can shape an answer but don't appear as links. If the question matches a workflow, the AI can point the resident to the form. On the phone, it can transfer the caller to a staff line or offer to text a link.

If the AI finds nothing useful, it says it can't help with that question rather than guessing. If a resident writes in a language your organization doesn't support, the AI replies with the languages it can help in.

## Example: a bulk-item pickup question

A resident in the Town of Fairview opens the chat on the public works page and asks, "When can I put out an old couch?"

1. The AI finds the town's *Bulk Item Collection* page and a *Collection zones* map layer.
2. Because pickup depends on the zone, the AI asks for the resident's address.
3. The resident types *412 Maple Ave*. The AI checks the map layer and finds Zone B.
4. The AI answers that Zone B bulk pickup is the first Monday of the month and links to the Bulk Item Collection page.

If the town later changes the schedule, staff update the web page. The next scheduled crawl picks up the change, and answers follow.
