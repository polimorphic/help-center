---
title: "Add GIS layers for location-based answers"
---

Some of the most common resident questions depend on where they live: "When is my trash day?" or "Which council district am I in?" Adding a map layer, such as trash collection zones, lets the AI ask for an address and give the right answer.

## Before you begin

- Get one of these from your [GIS](/getting-started/glossary) (geographic information system) team:
  - An ArcGIS FeatureServer or MapServer web address, or
  - A file: a ZIP of a shapefile, a GeoPackage (.gpkg), or GeoJSON, up to 100 MB.
- Make sure the layer has the field residents need answered, such as *pickup day* or *council district*.

## Describe the layer

1. Select **Settings** in the left menu.
2. Under **Build & Deploy**, select **GIS Layers**.
3. Select **Add GIS Layer**.
4. In **Name**, enter a clear name, such as *Trash and recycling zones*.
5. In **Description**, explain what questions the layer answers, such as *Use to find a resident's trash and recycling pickup day by address.* The AI reads this to decide when to use the layer.
6. Go to the next step.

## Connect the data

Under **How will this layer be connected?**, pick one option.

### Use a live map layer

1. Select **ArcGIS**.
2. In **ArcGIS URL**, paste the FeatureServer or MapServer address.
3. In **Layer**, select the layer you'd like to use.

### Upload a map file

1. Select **Upload GIS File**.
2. Pick your ZIP, GeoPackage, or GeoJSON file.
3. Wait while Polimorphic reads the file's fields. You can't continue until this finishes.

## Save and check the layer

1. Select **Save**.
2. In the list, select **View GIS layer on map** to confirm the shapes look right.

The layer appears on the **GIS Layers** tab with its **Geometry Type** and **Geometry Count**. When a resident asks a question the layer can answer, the AI asks for an address and checks the layer. If no layer matches a question, the AI doesn't ask for an address.

To test it, add a health check question such as *When is trash pickup at 412 Maple Ave?* and add the layer as a relevant source.
